v32 fails under the ramp: without a second pass, the shifted first passes leave the ramp's steep early decline unrefined (4 LRs: BS 2000 90.48, BS 500 92.46; conv only: BS 500 93.64, BS 125 93.54). Over its cells v32 is 0.097 pp short conv only and 0.55 pp with 4 LRs; v29's second pass, even when it can afford only its first interval, is needed.

A finer second-pass grid does not recover the grid's cost: paired with v29 on the same search seeds (which share their first passes), v33 scores 0.011 +- 0.007 pp lower (45 pairs); conv only it is 0.018 pp short of the baseline on average (11 of 12 within 0.1 pp) against v29's 0.016. Averaging two searches already puts the committed values between grid points wherever the searches disagree, and a finer second-pass climb gets single-seed decisions over smaller differences. v29 stays the algorithm.

Smoothing the committed values does not help either: replacing each interior value of v29's averaged 5-interval schedule by the log-space average 1/4, 1/2, 1/4 of it and its neighbours (endpoints kept; held-out probes of search seeds 0-3, 90 pairs) changes the held-out score by -0.016 +- 0.008 pp (38 of 90 higher). It helps without a transform (+0.032 pp; with 4 LRs at BS 2000 +0.108, to 93.85) and hurts under the wave (-0.074 pp; 4 LRs at BS 500 -0.151), whose optimum is genuinely non-monotone.

A cheaper 4-LR level scan costs more than it saves (ICS v34: the level candidates scored right after interval 1, without the one-interval cooldown, to leave the second passes more budget): paired with v29 it scores 0.106 +- 0.042 pp lower (24 searches; 5 of 12 cells within 0.1 pp). Without the cooldown the scan picks the lower level (60 instead of 460) in 11 of 24 searches, at BS 125 and 500 where v29 matched (BS 125 under the ramp 93.57), while the BS 2000 misses stay (93.75 and 93.78). The cooldown is what lets the short horizon rank these levels.

Scanning head's level apart from the biases' (ICS v35) fixes v29's two misses and breaks most other cells. At BS 2000 head lands one x7.7 step above the biases (3500 against 460) and the search scores 93.96 without a transform and 93.91 under the reverse ramp (v29: 93.76 and 93.76 over 8 search seeds; baseline 93.92). Elsewhere head runs to the top of what the short horizon accepts, 27000 or 210000, and the full run pays for it late: 92.27 at BS 2000 under the ramp, 93.19 at BS 500 under the reverse ramp, 73.3 in one BS 125 reverse-ramp search; paired with v29, -1.20 +- 0.86 pp (24 searches, 4 of 12 cells within 0.1 pp). Capping head at one x7.7 step above the common level would not help: head x7.7 on v29's schedules already loses 2-4 pp under the ramp (Experiment 25). With a short horizon, head's level can only be set safely together with the biases'; v29's two BS 2000 misses are the cost.
