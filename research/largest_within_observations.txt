Reference: Experiment 7's full-run 95% ranges for the best initial LR (conv 0.13-0.22, head 280-27000, norm_bias up to 3500, whiten_bias up to 5900; the last two open below, so for them only "above" is a real miss). A good short-horizon pick lands inside.

Conv: with the code's 2 pp margin, L = 1 and L = 2 pick above the range (0.36, 0.6) and L = 5, 10 and 40 inside it (0.13, 0.22, 0.13). So L = 1 does not predict L = 40's pick (2 grid steps apart, 0.36 vs 0.13), and the long-horizon picks are the ones that match the full run. With tighter margins it reverses: at 1 pp and 0.5 pp, L = 1 picks 0.22 (inside) and L = 5-40 pick 0.078 (one step below the range). No single margin puts every L inside.

Why the margin interacts with L: it is absolute, but the score scale is not (the centers score 25.43 at L = 1 and 84.60 at L = 40), and at every L the 2 pp pick sits 1-2 grid steps above that L's best-score center (L = 1: 0.13 -> 0.36; L = 40: 0.078 -> 0.13). The best-score centers alone are inside the range at L = 1 and 2 (0.13, 0.22) and one step below it at L >= 5 (0.078): short horizons favour slightly larger conv LRs than long ones, and the 2 pp rule's upward step compensates at L >= 5 but overshoots at L <= 2.

Head: L = 1 (770), 2 (460) and 40 (3500) pick inside the range; L = 5 (170) and 10 (36) below it, by 1-4 grid steps although the upward probe at L = 5 hit the 20-step limit. norm_bias and whiten_bias: their ranges are open below, so the only misses are picks above the upper edge: norm_bias at L = 5 (5900 > 3500), whiten_bias at L = 5 and 40 (9800 > 5900), each one grid step over. L = 1's picks (21 and 5900) are inside, but their ranges are wide enough that this says little.

So in this data L = 1 lands inside the full-run range for all three of head, norm_bias and whiten_bias (L = 40: two of three), but for conv, the one LR with a narrow range, L = 1 overshoots with the code's margin, while L >= 5 lands inside.

Caveat: in each L the other three LRs sit at that L's own Experiment 2 center, which differs between L (e.g. head 60 at L = 1, 0.006 at L = 5, 2100 at L = 40), so the comparison across L is not in a fixed context, and Experiment 7's context (full runs, baseline schedules) differs from all of them. Running the rule from a common start at every L would remove the first confound; it needs new runs.
