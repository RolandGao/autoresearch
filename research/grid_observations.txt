Five values joined linearly are enough: the oracle's 5 interval means, joined linearly, score at or above the baseline at BS 2000 in every cell (93.93-93.96) and within 0.08 pp of it at BS 500 and 125 (93.84-94.01), as good as 10 means. The grid is what costs: snapped to the x0.6 grid, the same 5 means lose 0.09 pp on average and up to 0.17 pp (BS 2000 under the ramp, 93.76), because a x0.6 step moves a value by up to x1.29 from where it should be. ICS v29 scores about as well as the grid-snapped oracle (BS 2000: 93.81-93.89 against 93.76-93.95), so its search is near the ceiling of what it commits; its two averaged searches already put values between grid points, but only where they disagree.
