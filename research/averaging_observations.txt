Averaging two search seeds' schedules helps on average: the averaged schedule beats the mean of its two parents by +0.066 pp over 26 cells (v1, v2 and v5, conv only), positive in 23 of them, by up to +0.18 pp (v2 at BS 125). It mostly recovers the better parent: when one seed made a costly mistake, the average sits between the two choices, and the score curves are flat enough near the optimum that the midpoint costs little.

This is decision noise being averaged out: the search's single-seed decisions err by a grid step in some intervals (Experiment 12), and two independent searches err in different intervals. Two v1 searches averaged (about 20 full runs together) reach 93.90 at BS 2000 without a transform and 93.89 under the reverse ramp, against 93.75 and 93.81 for one v5 search (about 15 full runs); averaging cannot fix a shared bias, though (v1 under the ramp stays at 93.16).
