A second pass closes most of the gap to the exhaustive joint search. With ICS's one-step rule the mean over the six transforms is 93.78 after one pass, 93.92 after two, and 93.99 for the joint search. With both sides + climb it is 93.74 and 93.90. Two one-step passes cost 14.8-16.8 full runs, against the joint search's 414-744. Every score is a single run on one seed (SD 0.12 pp per run), and the joint search picks the best of hundreds of runs, so its remaining lead is partly selection on noise.

Two one-step passes match or beat the joint search in three transforms. Under the ramp the result is 93.90 against 93.88: the schedule (0.36, 0.13, 0.078, 0.028, 0.0036) is one the joint search never trained. Under the wave and the inverted wave it is 93.93 and 94.03, tying the joint search; under the inverted wave the schedule is the joint winner itself. It falls 0.10 pp short without a transform and 0.14 pp short under the steep ramp, where it reaches the best schedule within one grid step of the plan (93.70). It falls 0.22 pp short under the reverse ramp, where the joint winner lies 2-3 grid steps below the plan late in the run.

The second pass helps where the first was misled by the plan's tail. Pass 1 scores each candidate with the plan's later values, so it cannot see that a later interval will move too. Pass 2 scores with pass 1's result instead. Pass 2 gained +0.04 to +0.38 pp in four transforms and changed nothing in the other two (none and the reverse ramp, already fixed points). It mostly kept pass 1's values: 25 of 30 one-step commitments kept them and 5 moved one step smaller. Its main fix is interval 2, from 0.13 to 0.078, under the ramp, the inverted wave and the steep ramp.

The wider rule does not help a single pass. Scoring both sides and climbing without a limit gave a mean of 93.74 against 93.78 for one step, at 9.6-11.8 runs against 6.6-8.4. It was better only under the reverse ramp (94.03 vs 93.95). It overshot early intervals under the ramp (interval 0 at 1.0, -0.11 pp), the steep ramp (interval 0 two steps down to 0.36, -0.16 pp) and the wave (interval 0 at 0.22, -0.05 pp). These early moves score best when the rest of the run follows the plan; the second pass moved all three back. The climb never went beyond two grid steps: in pass 1, 4 of 30 commitments moved two steps, 10 moved one and 16 kept the plan.

After two passes the rule hardly matters (93.92 vs 93.90). The second climbing pass undid the first pass's overshoots: the ramp's interval 0 went back to 0.6, the steep ramp's to 0.6, the wave's to 0.078. Under the reverse ramp the climb's two-step moves reached 94.10, 0.15 pp above one step and 0.07 pp below the joint search.

The training is deterministic: every refinement run scores exactly what Experiment 33 got for the same schedule, and the first pass of each 2-pass search reproduces the 1-pass search.
