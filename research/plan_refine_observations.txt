Refining one interval at a time loses 0.04-0.36 pp to the exhaustive joint search (mean 0.21 pp) with about 1% of its compute. By transform: none 93.99 vs 94.09, ramp 93.52 vs 93.88, reverse ramp 93.95 vs 94.17, wave 93.89 vs 93.93, inverted wave 93.81 vs 94.03, steep ramp 93.52 vs 93.84. The refinement used 6.6-8.4 full runs; the joint search used 414-744. Each score is the best of a handful (refinement) or hundreds (joint) of single runs on one seed, so the joint search's lead is partly selection on noise (SD 0.12 pp per run).

The refinement always improves on the plan (+0.08 to +0.70 pp) and on doing nothing (+0.07 to +1.05 pp). The training is deterministic: each of the 73 refinement runs scores exactly what Experiment 33 got for the same schedule.

The gap has two parts. The joint search's first iteration trained all 243 schedules within one grid step of the plan (the box). Summed over the six transforms, the box's limit costs 0.48 pp (joint winner minus best in the box) and greedy order 0.78 pp (best in the box minus refinement). Without a transform, under the wave and under the reverse ramp, the refinement found the box's best or came within 0.02 pp of it (3rd of 243). There, and under the steep ramp, the joint winner lies 2-3 grid steps below the plan late in the run, out of one step's reach: for example, a reverse-ramp tail of 0.047, 0.047, 0.017 against the plan's 0.13, 0.13, 0.078.

Greedy order costs the most under the ramps and the inverted wave. Under the ramp and the inverted wave the joint winner lies inside the box, and the refinement missed it (15th and 6th of 243, 0.36 and 0.22 pp short). Under the ramp, interval 1 committed 0.13 as soon as one step smaller beat the plan, so the one step larger (0.36, the winner's value) was never tried. Under the inverted wave, interval 2 kept 0.13 over the winner's 0.078 by 0.01 pp, a noise-level call scored with the plan's tail rather than the tail it then chose. Under the steep ramp the refinement differs from the box's best only in interval 2 (0.13 vs 0.078, 0.18 pp).

The candidate rule pulls toward smaller LRs: 17 of the 30 commitments are one step below the plan, 1 is one step above (interval 1, no transform), and 12 keep the plan. With smaller tried first and a stop at the first gain, a step down wins whenever it is any better.
