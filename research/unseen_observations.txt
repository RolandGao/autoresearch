v29 generalizes to the steeper ramp but not to the inverted wave. Under ramp8 it recovers most of what the transform costs (do-nothing 92.4-92.8, 1.1-1.6 pp below the baselines): conv only 93.83 / 93.92 / 93.79 at BS 2000 / 500 / 125, with 4 LRs 93.77 / 93.84 / 93.95, i.e. within 0.16 pp of the baseline everywhere and within 0.1 pp in 3 of 6 cells. Under the inverted wave it is 0.25-0.39 pp short conv only (93.53 / 93.73 / 93.61), barely above doing nothing (93.42 / 93.52 / 93.53), and 0.47 pp short with 4 LRs at BS 2000 (93.45).

The representation is not the problem: the oracle's 5 interval means joined linearly score 93.87-93.88 under the inverted wave, 93.74-93.86 snapped to the x0.6 grid. The search is: the oracle holds interval 2 as high as interval 1 (BS 2000: 0.29, 0.29, 0.12, 0.037, 0.014) and falls only after the wave's trough, while v29's first pass already drops at interval 2 (0.13-0.17; at BS 500 0.061-0.078 against 0.17), 1.5-2 grid steps too low: the short cooldown's small-LR bias, larger here because interval 2 lies in the trough (x0.52-0.67). Its second pass does not undo this. Allowing its climbs two grid steps (v43) changes nothing (paired with v29 -0.008 +- 0.008 pp on these cells, +0.000 on the 24 standard ones): the climbs never take a second step, because a candidate is scored with the rest of the run held at the plan's low values, against which raising interval 2 alone does not pay.

Scoring the second pass's candidates with the rest of the run decaying linearly from the candidate (v44, so the tail rises with it) does not fix it either: under the held-back transforms it scores as v29 (paired -0.002 +- 0.005 pp conv only, +0.000 with 4 LRs), and on the 24 standard cells it is a wash (conv only +0.017 +- 0.009 pp, 12 of 12 within 0.1 pp; 4 LRs -0.010 +- 0.011 pp, 9 of 12). The inverted wave's loss is made in the first pass, whose short cooldown sets interval 2 too low in the trough; a uniform larger correction of that bias loses elsewhere (Experiment 24). v29 stays the algorithm; the inverted wave is its known failure case.
