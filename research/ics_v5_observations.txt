v5 is v2 made cheaper at the start and less noisy at the end, and it is no better on average: 93.58-93.81 at BS 2000 (baseline 93.92), 93.65-93.86 at BS 500 (94.00) and 93.42-93.72 at BS 125 (93.86), for 14-20 full runs. Its best single result is the C4 schedule itself (0.13, 0.13, 0.078, 0.047, 0.01; 93.95 at BS 2000, search seed 0), so the method can find a matching schedule but does not reliably.

At BS 500 and 125 it is systematically too low: its BS 125 schedules (e.g. 0.028, 0.017, 0.006, 0.0036, 0.0013) sit one to two grid steps below the 5-interval staircase that scores 93.80 (0.04, 0.031, 0.021, 0.012, 0.0024), and its BS 500 schedules start at 0.078 where the staircase starts at 0.11. The first pass's short cooldown picks low, and the second pass does not raise it, probably because its candidates scale the whole remaining plan, so raising a middle interval also raises the end of the run, which was already right (v10 tests this by keeping the tail unscaled).

Replicating the last two intervals' decisions did not show a measurable gain over v2 (same cells, 2 search seeds each), which is consistent with the late decisions costing little either way (Experiment 12).
