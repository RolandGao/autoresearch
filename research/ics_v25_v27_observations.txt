Averaging two independent half-budget searches removes search noise but not the gap. Conv only, v26 is 0.073 pp short of the baseline on average over the 12 cells (8 within 0.1 pp, worst BS 2000 under the reverse ramp at 93.71), against 0.081 pp for v16 and 0.106 pp for v19 over its 8 cells with 4 seeds. Yet the spread between its two search seeds is half v19's: the mean within-cell SD of the held-out score is 0.041 pp for v26 against 0.079 pp for v19. What is left is the same in every search: a systematic error, not noise. BS 125 is v26's best batch size (93.87 / 93.76 / 93.83 / 93.84 without a transform / ramp / reverse ramp / wave; baseline 93.86).

With 4 LRs, the two searches cannot both afford the level scan (about 4.8 full runs at BS 500): in v26 the second pass of each search ran out of budget before its first decision. v27 scans once and shares the levels, but is no better than v24: 0.086 pp short on average (6 of 12 within 0.1 pp) against 0.081 pp (7 of 12). It gains under the reverse ramp at BS 2000 (93.84 vs 93.64) and loses without a transform at BS 2000 (93.68 vs 93.82) and BS 500 (93.95 vs 94.06); with each search's second pass cut to a few decisions, the averaging buys stability at the cost of refinement.

v25 (the second pass refines every interval but the first) is 0.094 pp short over its 4 cells; it is the only version near the baseline at BS 2000 under the reverse ramp (93.86, 2 seeds; v19 93.77 over 4) but is lower at BS 125 without a transform (93.77 vs 93.83).

The searched LRs do lie below the oracle from the middle of the run on: across all 175 conv-only 10-interval schedules searched since v9, log2(searched / oracle) is -0.4 to -1.1 in intervals 5-9 at BS 2000 without a transform and -0.8 to -2.2 at BS 500 under the ramp; and relative to the shifted plan, the second pass's climbs keep the value in 66% of 342 decisions, step down in 27% and up in only 7%. But Experiment 24 shows that scaling the searched schedules up loses on held-out seeds; the systematic error is the staircase shape, not the scale.
