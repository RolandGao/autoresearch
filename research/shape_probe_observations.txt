The searched schedules are not too small: scaling them up loses. Over the 14 search seeds probed so far (BS 2000 and BS 500), x1.67 everywhere costs 0.143 +- 0.050 pp (3 of 14 higher), x2.78 costs 0.69 +- 0.10 pp (none higher), and the graded x1 to x2.8 costs 0.128 +- 0.044 pp (3 of 14 higher). Only the ramp cells gain from x1.67 (BS 500: 93.88 / 93.84 to 93.97 / 93.98), where the oracle's early LRs are 3-4x the baseline's. The correlation of Experiment 23 (larger than the cell's average, higher score) does not hold up as a cause: it compares searches that differ in more than scale.

The staircase is what costs. The oracle's 10 interval means as constant steps score 93.92 at BS 2000 but 93.86 at BS 500, 0.14 pp below the baseline they come from; joined linearly they score at or above the baseline in every cell (93.88-94.02; 0.071 pp above the steps on average over the 8 cells with both). The same holds for the searched schedules: joined linearly, v26's conv-only schedules score 0.028 +- 0.013 pp higher than as steps (15 of 24 search seeds), and v27's 4-LR schedules 0.050 +- 0.015 pp higher (17 of 24).

Joining the averaged 5-interval schedule directly (without the expansion to 10 intervals) is as good conv only (+0.020 +- 0.016 pp, 14 of 24) and better with 4 LRs (+0.085 +- 0.017 pp, 21 of 24): as cell means over search seeds 0-1, v27's 4-LR search is then 0.001 pp short of the baseline on average (9 of 12 cells within 0.1 pp; worst BS 2000 without a transform, 93.70), against 0.086 pp short as steps; conv only, v26 is 0.054 pp short (9 of 12; 0.046 and 10 of 12 joined from 10 intervals) against 0.073 pp. A linear interpolation of 5 values is smoother than the expansion's 10 tilted steps; with 4 LRs the head and bias levels decay in the same shape, so all four groups gain.
