Where the mandate stands. Within the 20-run budget, v29 matches the baseline (within 0.1 pp of the held-out mean) in all 12 conv-only cells at all three batch sizes and under all three transforms, over 12 search seeds (0.023 pp short on average, worst 0.08 pp) and again on fresh seeds 200-207 (Experiment 30). With all 4 LRs searched from 1.0 it is 0.012 pp short on average and matches in 9 of 12 cells over 12 search seeds (8 of 12 on seeds 200-207); the cells it misses are BS 2000 without a transform (93.74, 0.18 pp short) and under the reverse ramp (93.81) and BS 500 under the reverse ramp (93.89), the last two by 0.11 pp; on the fresh seeds BS 2000 under the ramp also falls 0.11 pp short.

What the 4-LR misses are, and what did not fix them: they come from setting head's and the biases' levels with a short horizon. One common level, the largest within 1 pp after a one-interval cooldown, is right on average (moving head alone x0.36 or x2.78 loses 0.1 pp, Experiment 25), but at BS 2000 head wants a level above the biases' (head x7.7 scores 93.91 / 93.83 without a transform). Scanning head on its own (v35) fixes BS 2000 and wrecks the ramp cells, because a short horizon accepts head levels that blow up late in the run; a rest-of-run head relevel (v31) finds no budget; lowering the common level (v42), a cheaper scan (v34), smoothing, a linear decay for the other groups, a finer second-pass grid (v33), a third search (v32), a trend-started first pass (v38) and 4 or 8 intervals (v40, v41) do not close the gap (Experiments 27-29). Conv only, the search is at the ceiling of what 5 grid values joined linearly can represent (Experiment 26).

Generality: under two transforms held back until v29 was final (Experiment 31, 8 search seeds) it matches in only 3 of 12 cells. It handles a steeper ramp conv only (within 0.14 pp) but not with 4 LRs at BS 2000 and 500 (0.45 and 0.19 pp short), and it fails under the inverted wave conv only (0.18-0.30 pp short, barely above doing nothing) and with 4 LRs at BS 2000 (0.74 pp short), because its first pass's short cooldown sets the interval in the wave's trough too low and the second pass cannot raise it against the plan's tail.
