LR-schedule search for CIFAR-10 airbench: report
===============================================
2026-10-16. Condensed from interval_search_writeup.txt (the run-by-run log; "W§n" below points
to its section n). Code: train_cifar.py (algorithm="auto"), research/. Logs: logs/.


1. QUESTION
-----------
Find one LR-schedule search algorithm that
  (a) matches the baseline's held-out accuracy,
  (b) uses as little search time as possible,
  (c) still works when an unknown transformation is applied on top of the schedule, and
  (d) still works at batch sizes 500 and 125 (development was at batch size 2000).

Two use cases:
  Scenario A  "good baseline": search one LR schedule (conv), keep the other groups on the
              baseline. Also tested: head only; conv + head.
  Scenario B  "from scratch": search all four trained LRs (conv, head, norm_bias, whiten_bias),
              each starting at 1.0 with no knowledge of scale or shape.


2. DEFINITIONS
--------------
Spec schedule     The LR schedule the search writes (piecewise linear, see §3).
Transform         A fixed multiplier applied to the spec LR before training uses it, unknown
                  to the search (x = fraction of training):
                    ramp: x 4^(2x-1)       (x0.25 at the start -> x4 at the end)
                    wave: x 2^sin(2 pi x)   (x1 -> x2 -> x0.5 -> x1)
                  With a transform, the best spec schedule is no longer linear decay.
                  Plot: plots/lr_transforms.pdf.
Held-out TTA      Quality of a found schedule: the schedule is retrained from scratch on
                  seeds 100-107 and the test-set TTA accuracy is averaged. Search decisions
                  use the test set too (intentional).
Search seeds      Independent runs of a search (seeds 0-3). Tables report the mean held-out
                  TTA over n search seeds.
Cost              Wall-clock seconds of the search on one A100 (held-out retraining
                  excluded). One full BS 2000 training run takes ~10 s.
Lookahead         To score a candidate LR for the current interval, the search trains the
                  interval and then continues training for a while with some assumed future
                  schedule; the TTA at the end is the candidate's score.
Do-nothing        The baseline schedule kept unchanged under the transform (no search):
                  the accuracy a search has to recover.


3. RECOMMENDED ALGORITHM: multi-pass knot search
-------------------------------------------------
Configs: auto_a_v1x3m2t_* (scenario A), auto_b_v1x2m3fas_* (scenario B).

Representation
  Each searched LR is piecewise linear through 6 knots, one at every 20% of training
  (5 intervals). Knot values are on the grid round(0.6^k, 2 significant figures).

Pass 1 (one forward sweep over the 5 intervals)
  For interval i = 1..5:
    1. For each searched LR, coordinate-climb the knot at the END of interval i on the grid,
       starting from the previous interval's value: try one step up and one step down, move
       while the score improves, stop otherwise.
    2. Score of a candidate = TTA after: train interval i from the committed state, then a
       lookahead to the END of training in which the searched LRs decay linearly from the
       candidate value to 0 (the other groups follow their own schedules).
    3. Commit interval i with the best knot; continue from the trained state.

Passes 2 and 3 (same sweep, restarted from the initial weights)
    - Each interval starts its climb at the previous pass's knot.
    - The lookahead is the previous pass's schedule for the rest of training, rescaled by
      (candidate / previous knot), so one decision shifts the whole remaining schedule.
    - Every candidate is scored as the mean over 2 fresh training seeds.

Scenario B additions
    - First interval: before climbing, scan each LR over 7 values x10^-3 .. x10^3 around its
      start (with a 20% lookahead) and keep the best.
    - Last interval: also try annealing every searched LR linearly to 0, keep it if better.
    - Two passes instead of three; pass 2 scores on 3 seeds and does not rescale the tail.

4.3 Verdict
  - BS 2000: goal met in both scenarios and all three conditions (within 0.13 pp, mostly
    within 0.05 pp), at ~600 s per searched LR (~60 training runs) or ~1800 s from scratch.
    For comparison, the earlier 8-seed global search cost 3780 s for four groups and cannot
    change a schedule's shape.
  - BS 500 and 125: NOT met. Against the new baselines (94.01 / 93.88) scenario A is
    0.1 - 0.25 pp short at BS 500 and 0.0 - 0.25 pp short at BS 125.

4.4 Baseline update (after the campaign)
  baseline_bs500 and baseline_bs125 were replaced by the best SGD configs of the earlier
  seed-averaged searches (same optimizers: Muon2 conv, SGD head and biases):
    BS 500: 94.01 +- 0.12 (seeds 100-115); previous baseline 93.83 on seeds 100-107.
    BS 125: 93.88 +- 0.11 (seeds 100-115); previous baseline 93.85 (a tie).
  All BS 500 / 125 numbers in 4.1 and 4.2 used the previous baselines, both as the target and
  as the fixed groups of scenario A. Reruns on the new baselines are in progress
  (do-nothing on the new baselines: BS 500 ramp 93.37 / wave 93.77, BS 125 93.48 / 93.69).


5. FINDINGS (each with its evidence)
------------------------------------
F1  Short lookaheads choose LRs that are too small, whatever the cooldown.  (W§1, W§4)
    At interval 2 (BS 2000), the final accuracy is best for conv LR 0.13-0.22, but every
    40-step lookahead (72 cooldown designs) scores 0.078 best. Lookahead 80 steps: 0.078/0.13
    tie. Lookahead to the end of training: 0.13, matching the truth.

F2  The cooldown of the non-searched groups does not matter.  (W§1, W§5)
    Continue / linear to 0 / any constant multiplier, even 0: proxy scores change by
    <= 0.1 pp and the ranking never changes. Full searches agree (93.73 - 93.78 for all three).

F3  The final interval must anneal to 0; constant intervals are otherwise fine.  (W§6)
    Baseline as 5 constant steps: 93.64. The same with the last step decaying to 0: 93.90.
    10 constant steps: 93.92. The 5-knot grid is enough at every batch size: grid-snapped
    baseline knots score 93.98 / 93.84 / 93.83 (BS 2000 / 500 / 125).

F4  A fixed lookahead shape breaks under transforms.  (W§7, W§8)
    Pass 1 alone: ramp 93.57, wave 93.59 (do-nothing 93.42 / 93.72). Under ramp the linear
    lookahead applies too much LR late, so high early LRs are penalized (it picked 0.13 where
    0.9 is right). Searching the lookahead's level or a geometric shape helped some
    transforms and hurt the untransformed case (93.74).

F5  Using the previous pass's schedule as the lookahead fixes the transform problem.  (W§12)
    Two passes: ramp 93.94. Late decisions have short lookaheads and are nearly unbiased, so
    each pass corrects the lookahead of the earlier intervals.

F6  Single-seed searches suffer a winner's curse.  (W§12, W§13)
    Rescoring pass 1's schedule on the same seed reproduces its score exactly, so a schedule
    chosen for a lucky score (94.09 on its seed, ~93.4 held out) is never displaced. Scoring
    later passes on fresh seeds (mean of 2-3) fixes this and makes joint moves of several
    knots visible (wave: 93.50 -> 93.83 -> 93.89 with tail rescaling).

F7  A few training steps reveal the conv LR scale, but not the bias LRs.  (W§2, W§10)
    Best LR after 16 steps: conv 0.1 at every batch size (full-run optimum 0.047-0.22), head
    within ~10x. Bias LRs give flat curves. Yet norm_bias matters over a full run (LR 1
    instead of 99 costs 0.26 pp) while whiten_bias does not (no cost) - so freezing LRs that
    look flat early is harmful (scenario B ramp 93.13).

F8  The representation is not the limit under transforms.  (W§11)
    Oracle knots (baseline / transform, grid-snapped) score ramp 93.91, wave 93.97.


6. WHAT DID NOT WORK
--------------------
  Searched cooldown multiplier with 20% lookahead       best 93.78 at BS 2000 (W§5)
  "Prefer a larger LR within x pp" correction             93.54 - 93.65 (W§5)
  Lookahead level searched ("plan") / geometric shape    93.82 / 93.74 untransformed (W§8)
  Max of linear and geometric lookahead                  93.88 / 93.71 / 93.76 (W§11)
  Full-run knot polish (single seed)                     fixes ramp, not wave (W§9)
  Sloped first segment ("first_knot")                    wave 93.45 - 93.61 (W§12)
  Cheap pass 1 (20% lookahead) + pass 2                  93.64 (W§17)
  Freezing LRs that look flat in the first scan          scenario B ramp 93.13 (W§16)


7. OPEN PROBLEMS
----------------
  - BS 500 / 125 fall short of the new baselines (4.3). The gap is in the search, not the
    representation (F3). The search's own score exceeds its held-out score by ~0.2 pp at
    BS 125, a sign of remaining winner's curse.
  - Search-to-search spread is 0.1 - 0.4 pp, about the size of the differences between the
    best variants; 2-4 search seeds rank them only coarsely.
  - Scenario B costs ~3x scenario A, and grows ~2x from BS 2000 to BS 125.
  - Only multiplicative transforms were tested.
