Interval search research log (2026-10-16, 12-hour autonomous study)
====================================================================

Goal
----
Find one general LR-schedule search algorithm that uses the least total search time while
matching the baseline's held-out TTA, that survives simple transformations applied on top
of the specified schedule (so the best specified schedule is not linear decay), and that
survives batch size 500 and 125. Two scenarios:
  A. A good baseline exists: search a few LR schedules (here: conv), keep the rest fixed.
  B. From scratch: search every trained LR (conv, head, norm_bias, whiten_bias), starting
     at 1.0 with no knowledge of their scale or shape.
Selection and evaluation are on the CIFAR-10 test set (intentional).

Protocol
--------
- Code: train_cifar.py (run_auto_search, algorithm="auto"; SegmentTrainer; lr_transform),
  research/*.py (probes and analysis), logs/ (every run, with its source / commit).
- Development at BS 2000 (200 steps); BS 500 (800) and BS 125 (3200) are validation.
- Cost = wall-clock seconds of the search on one A100 (search_seconds in the log; the
  committed run is part of it). Held-out evaluation is not part of the cost.
- Quality = held-out TTA of the committed schedule: the schedule is retrained from scratch
  on seeds 100-107 (heldout_result line), averaged over search seeds where available.
- Transforms (lr_transform, applied to the searched groups): ramp = x 4**(2t/T - 1)
  (x0.25 -> x4 over the run), wave = x 2**sin(2 pi t/T). Under a transform, keeping the
  linear-decay spec gives a distorted applied schedule; the best spec is non-linear.

Targets (held-out TTA, current code)
------------------------------------
baseline_bs2000: 93.92 +- 0.13 (seeds 100-107), 93.96 +- 0.12 (seeds 108-123) -> 93.94 (24 seeds)
baseline_bs500:  93.83 +- 0.12 (seeds 100-107)
baseline_bs125:  93.85 +- 0.11 (seeds 100-107)
Single-run seed sd ~0.12-0.13; an 8-seed mean has sd ~0.045.

Results
-------
(sections 1-18 in the order they were run; section 19 at the end is the summary and recommendation)

   lookahead under 72 cooldown designs (searched: m_s x c or linear c->0; others: continue,
   linear ->0, or m_o x their main-interval mean).
   k=1 (steps 40-80), seed-mean TTA per candidate conv LR:
                    0.028  0.047  0.078   0.13   0.22   0.36    0.6
     truth          93.63  93.73  93.83  93.94  93.98  93.57  93.26
     no cooldown    90.68  90.65  89.66  87.25  81.99  72.31  55.96
     s0.22|cont     91.74  92.36  92.66  92.55  91.64  89.37  86.07

   - Every 40-step lookahead peaks at 0.078; the truth peaks at 0.13-0.22. The bias toward
     small LRs (short-horizon bias) is ~1-1.5 grid steps and no cooldown design removes it.
   - The non-searched groups' cooldown (continue / linear / any m_o, even 0) changes the
     proxy by <= ~0.1 pp and never the ranking: when conv is searched, the other groups'
     cooldown does not matter (they are insensitive hparams).

     and closest to the truth; 1.0 (no cooldown of the searched LR) is as bad as none.

   - Usable as a cheap start for scenario B (conv, head); not for the bias LRs.

6. The real cap: the final interval must anneal (fixed schedules, conv, seeds 100-107)
     staircase (n constant steps at the mean of the baseline's linear decay in each step)
     n=5  93.64 | n=5 grid-snapped 93.52 | n=10 93.92 | n=20 93.95
     n=5 but the last step decays linearly (same mean, 0.045 -> 0)      93.90
     n=5 but the last step is the baseline's own (0.044 -> 0)           93.91
     all four groups as n=5 staircases: 93.68 (grid-snapped 93.64)
   - Constant intervals cost nothing except at the end: a constant final interval
     (no anneal to 0) costs ~0.28 pp, and the 5-interval searches above were capped by it.
   - Fix under test: final_anneal ("linear": last interval decays to 0 from its searched
     value; "search": try constant and annealed, keep the better), shape="linear"
     (piecewise-linear knots), and 10 intervals.

7. v1 = piecewise-linear knots + lookahead to the end decaying linearly to 0 (scenario A, BS 2000)
     no transform: 93.94 held-out (2 search seeds: 93.95, 93.93), 132 s, 21.5 evaluations
       -> matches the baseline (93.94). final_anneal on constant intervals: 93.86-93.91.
     ramp:  93.57 (do-nothing 93.42; baseline 93.92) -> recovers only 0.15 of 0.50 pp
     wave:  93.59 (do-nothing 93.72)                 -> worse than not searching
   Why: the lookahead's linear decay is specified in spec space, so under a transform its
   applied shape is distorted (ramp: the applied LR rises late in the lookahead), which
   penalizes high-LR candidates (ramp: chose 0.13-0.22 where the compensating spec is ~0.9).
   Only "decays to 0 by the end" survives a multiplicative transform. Next (v2): search the
   lookahead's level ("plan": an absolute start level per group, carried between intervals;
   "linsearch": a searched mult x candidate LR), still decaying linearly to 0 by the end.

9. Polish: full-run knot coordinate search after the online pass (v1p; margin 0.1 pp, <= 3 rounds)
   Each candidate moves one knot of the piecewise-linear schedule one x0.6 grid step and is
   trained from scratch to the end (same seed), accepted if it beats the best by > 0.1 pp;
   the accepted run's own model is the result.
                              none            ramp            wave
     v1   (online only)       93.94 / 132 s   93.57 / 150 s   93.59 / 128 s
     v1p  (online + polish)   93.93 / 267 s   93.92 / 369 s   93.59 / 259 s
     BS 500 v1p               93.81 / 335 s   93.80 / 405 s         (baseline_bs500 93.83)
   - Polish fixes the ramp failure (v1's lookahead too high late -> knots 1-3 raised by one
     step each in round 0) and leaves the no-transform result at the baseline.
   - It does not fix wave: the online pass left knots 1 and 3 two to three grid steps too
     high, and a one-knot, one-step move gains less than the single-seed noise (~0.17 pp),
     so no move clears the margin. Polish needs a starting point within ~1 step.
   - Next: v3p = the transform-adaptive online pass (geometric lookahead) + polish.

12. Two passes, and the winner's curse (scenario A, BS 2000)
    passes=2: the second pass starts each interval at the first pass's knot and uses the first
    pass's schedule as its lookahead (late decisions, whose lookaheads are short and barely
    distorted, correct the lookahead of early ones).
      v1x2  ramp 93.94 (2 seeds, 272 s: early knots raised to 0.6, 0.6, 0.22 ~ oracle 1.0, 0.36, 0.22)
      v1x2  wave 93.50 (2 seeds, 229 s): pass 2 made no move at all.
    Why: pass 2's centre evaluation replays pass 1's run on the same seed exactly, so it
    inherits pass 1's selected (lucky) score; one run had locked in a flat 0.13 schedule
    scoring 94.09 on its own seed (~93.4 on held-out seeds), and fresh-trajectory neighbours
    almost never beat a lucky incumbent. The same lock-in explains why polish stalls under
    wave. first_knot (sloped first segment) did not help (wave 93.61 / 93.45 with polish).
    Fix under test: reseed (each later pass / polish round re-scores the incumbent on a fresh seed).

16. plan_tail: pass-2 candidates scale the plan's whole remaining schedule (v1x2m3t)
    A candidate for an interval's end knot multiplies the rest of the plan by
    candidate / plan value, and the committed choice keeps that scaled tail, so each pass-2
    decision moves a whole region of the schedule (a larger, more detectable effect); a
    later interval can still move back (wave: lower knots 1-2, then raise knot 3).
      BS 2000 (2 search seeds): none 94.05 (457 s)  ramp 93.90 (509 s)  wave 93.86 (493 s)
      BS 125 wave: 93.75 (912 s; v1x2m3 93.50, do-nothing 93.68, baseline 93.85)
    Freezing LRs that look flat in the short first-interval scan is harmful from scratch:
    norm_bias looks flat over a 20% horizon but matters over the run (frozen at 1000; the
    right value is ~100, ~400 -> ~25 under ramp): v1x2m2fasff ramp 93.13 (range 0.78).

19. SUMMARY AND RECOMMENDATION (final, 2026-10-16 ~18:00 UTC; held-out TTA = schedule retrained
    on seeds 100-107, mean over search seeds; cost = search wall-clock on one A100)
    ===========================================================================================
    Recommended algorithm: multi-pass knot search (train_cifar.py, algorithm="auto"; configs
    auto_a_v1x3m2t_* for a good baseline, auto_b_v1x2m3fas_* from scratch).
      1. Representation: each searched LR is piecewise linear through knots at every 20% of the
         run (5 intervals) on the x0.6 grid. This is enough at every batch size: grid-snapped
         baseline knots score 93.98 / 93.84 / 93.83 at BS 2000 / 500 / 125 (baselines 93.94 /
         93.83 / 93.85), and oracle knots under the transforms score 93.91 (ramp) / 93.97 (wave).
      2. Pass 1 (online): for each interval in order, coordinate-climb each searched LR's end
         knot on the grid starting from the previous value. Score a candidate by training the
         interval from the committed state, then a lookahead to the END of the run in which the
         searched LRs decay linearly to 0 (the other groups continue their own schedules).
         Commit the best and move on.
      3. Passes 2..P (P = 3): restart from the initial state. Each interval starts at the
         previous pass's knot; the lookahead is the previous pass's schedule, its tail scaled by
         candidate / plan value; every candidate is scored by the mean over 2 fresh seeds.
      4. From scratch only: in the first interval scan each LR over +-3 decades (x10 steps,
         20% lookahead) before refining, and final_anneal="search" (try annealing all searched
         LRs to 0 in the last interval).
    Why each piece (sections): a lookahead to the end of the run, because short lookaheads
    prefer small LRs by 1-1.5 grid steps whatever the cooldown (1, 4, 5), while the other
    groups' cooldown never matters (1, 5); knots that anneal to 0, because a constant final
    interval costs 0.28 pp (6, 7); extra passes, because the fixed linear lookahead is
    distorted by transforms and the previous pass's schedule is a self-correcting lookahead
    (8, 12); fresh multi-seed scoring, because single-seed searches lock in lucky schedules
    (winner's curse) and cannot see joint knot moves smaller than the per-knot noise (12, 13);
    tail scaling, because each decision then moves a whole region (16); a third pass, because
    under ramp the second pass still left the early knots too low (search seeds 93.58-94.03).

      BS 500:  none 93.89 (1, 2471s), ramp 93.90 (1, 2698s)
    Verdict against the goal (match the baseline's held-out TTA at least cost, survive the
    transforms and BS 500 / 125):
      - BS 2000: matched in every condition (93.88-93.96 vs 93.94) in ~600 s per searched LR,
        i.e. ~60 training runs; from scratch ~1800 s (~180 runs). The older seed-averaged
        global search cost 3780 s for four groups and cannot reshape a transformed schedule.
      - BS 500: within 0.05-0.1 pp (93.75-93.91 vs 93.83) in ~700 s.
      - BS 125: 0.0-0.25 pp short (93.59-93.88 vs 93.85, single seeds) in ~1100 s; the shortfall
        is in the search (the representation reaches 93.83), so it is not yet solved.
    Limits and open problems:
      - Search-seed spread is 0.1-0.4 pp, about the size of the differences between the best
        variants; two to four search seeds rank them only coarsely.
      - Cost grows with batch-size steps (BS 125 ~2x BS 2000) and with the number of searched
        groups (four groups ~3x one). Cheaper first passes (20% lookahead) and freezing
        "flat-looking" LRs both failed: a short horizon misjudges long-horizon LRs (norm_bias).
      - The algorithm assumes nothing about the schedule's shape except piecewise linearity on
        a 20% grid and (from scratch) the option to anneal to 0 at the end; it was tested on
        multiplicative transforms (ramp x0.25 -> x4, wave x2**sin), not on others.
